## What the video adds beyond the page text

These points are quoted from the transcript below; the page itself does not say them.

- **Exploration operations as a dimension:** "the landing the navigation mobility robotics constructions resource utilizations or even human operations in general". This matches the mission-objective framing in ADR-0002.
- **Named data sources:** "nasa Earth data", "world View", "nasa's planetary data system", "lunar reconnaissance orbiter data can tell you about lunar topography temperatures surface properties", and "data sets from other agencies".
- **Known analogs, a longer list than the page gives:** "Atakama desert Antarctica Iceland Hawaii the Mojave Desert the Death Valley and the HTON impact crater".
- **"Where it breaks down" framed as part of the science:** "tell us where the analogy works how well it works and where it breaks down". The limits named are lunar gravity, vacuum, Martian atmospheric conditions and the radiation environment of either world.
- **Systematic search invited explicitly:** "develop a map a similarity metric an interactive application or machine learning approach that systematically searches Earth for previously unrecognized planetary analoges".

## Transcript (verbatim)

i am Sidameit Bukabara from NASA Earth Science Division and I'm the challenge author for the NASA space app challenge that's called identify Earth locations that analog the permanent moon base locations and Mars

now as humanity prepares to return to the moon through the NASA Artemis campaign that you probably have heard about establishing a sustained human presence and establishing a permanent base on the moon and eventually explore Mars we face a fascinating question that I would like you to contribute answering for us that question is what can our own planet Earth teach us about exploring other worlds and in particular the moon and planet Mars

as you know Earth is the planet we know best and across Earth we find extraordinary environments deserts polar regions volcanoes impact craters mountains caves and lava tubes extreme cold temperatures dust ice and exposed geological formations we all find that on Earth some of these environments share important characteristics with places on the moon or Mars and that is where the challenge begins

we want you to use NASA observations either the Earth the Moon or Mars data sets to search with us our planet for locations environments or even physical processes that could help us prepare for Artemis the future permanent moonbased operations and eventually the exploration of Mars

but there is an important twist to this we are not simply asking you to find a place that just looks like the moon or Mars we want you to think scientifically what we mean by that is try to perhaps find a location that has similar geology or minerals perhaps it has extreme temperatures unusual illumination ice perafrost perhaps it has dust processes such as dust storms volcanic terrain caves or underground environments or perhaps its terrain provides a useful environment for testing the things that will actually be needed to establish and operate a lunar base the landing the navigation mobility robotics constructions resource utilizations or even human operations in general

and keep in mind no analog has the has to be perfect in fact none of those analoges will be perfect earth cannot reproduce lunar gravity and vacuum Martian atmospheric conditions or the radiation environment of either world so we are not asking you to find the ideal or the perfect analog part of your scientific challenge is therefore to tell us where the analogy works how well it works and where it breaks down and doesn't work

you probably know that we already know some famous planetary analoges such as the Atakama desert Antarctica Iceland Hawaii the Mojave Desert the Death Valley and the HTON impact crater but haven't we discovered other things what haven't we discovered yet

nasa observes the entire Earth that gives you an enormous laboratory to explore you could investigate one remarkable location you could compare locations around the world or you could go much further and develop a map a similarity metric an interactive application or machine learning approach that systematically searches Earth for previously unrecognized planetary analoges that we don't know about

you have extraordinary data available to you nasa Earth data is an example world View is another one they provide observations of our planet nasa's planetary data system gives you access to observations of the moon and Mars lunar reconnaissance orbiter data can tell you about lunar topography temperatures surface properties and other characteristics and data sets from other agencies other space agencies can expand your search even further

so don't just search for something that somebody has already found we want you to surprise us find an unexpected place discover an unexpected connection or invent a new way of comparing Earth with the moon and Mars because as Artemis takes us back to the moon and toward a sustained human presence there and ultimately on Mars some of the knowledge that helps us explore another world may be waiting to be discovered right here on our own planet Earth

good luck and we look forward to seeing what you have to discover and share with us
