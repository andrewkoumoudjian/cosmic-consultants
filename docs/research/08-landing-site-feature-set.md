# 08 — Landing-site feature set v0 (Moon, Mars) and Earth counterparts

Date: 2026-11-12. Issue #29. Status: **research — proposed**. Nothing here sets an
ARL tolerance; W5 (#18) builds envelopes and may cite these rows.

Machine-readable list: [`research/features/landing_site_features.csv`](../../research/features/landing_site_features.csv)
(55 features), generated by `research/features/build_feature_list.py`. Edit the
builder, not the CSV.

## 1. Shape: a characteristic list, as in a factor panel

The format follows an equity characteristics panel, where one row is a security-month
and the columns are coded characteristics such as `ret_12_1` or `ivol_capm_21d`. Here:

- **Unit of observation:** one `(cell, body)` row. Cells are Earth H3 cells or Moon/Mars
  site cells, with the same feature codes on both sides wherever the physics allows.
- **Code = measure + scale.** For example, `slope_100m` is the slope over a 100 m
  baseline, and `slope_2_5km` is the slope over a 2–5 km baseline. A different
  baseline gives a different feature. This matters because MSL alone sets four
  different slope limits at four length scales.
- **Each row is a feature card.** It gives the definition, unit, scale, target-body
  source (a registry `datasetId` from #4 where one exists), the published
  engineering threshold with a source key, the Earth counterpart feature and
  dataset, and `earth_match`.
- **No composite.** Features feed per-dimension losses only (AGENTS.md rule 4).
  `mismatch` rows are never scored. They populate the "Earth cannot rehearse this"
  list (S1 / S6f).

`earth_match` values:

| Value | Meaning | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| `direct` | Same quantity and physics on Earth (e.g. DEM slope, LST, TIR emissivity, VSWIR band depths) | 21 |
| `proxy` | Related physics or a different unit (e.g. apparent thermal inertia vs. thermal inertia; permafrost probability vs. ice-stability depth) | 15 |
| `weak` | Only loosely analogous (e.g. Earth horizon masking vs. the Earth-visibility comms constraint) | 4 |
| `gap` | No global Earth dataset (e.g. rock abundance, 2–5 m slopes, Lyman-α frost, gamma-ray Th/K) | 9 |
| `mismatch` | Permanent breakdown: gravity, pressure/EDL atmosphere, radiation, ~100 K cold traps, vertical EDL winds | 6 |

The `gap` rows matter for scoring. A dimension that depends on them cannot pass ARL
gates 1–3 from global data. Those features need site evidence (gate 4, literature or
fieldwork), which is the honest version of "not well characterized" (brief F4).

## 2. Families

| Family | What defines a site | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering: terrain | Can the lander touch down and the rover or crew move? | `slope_2_5km`, `slope_200_500m`, `slope_20_40m`, `slope_5m` (Mars); `slope_100m`, `slope_2km_eva` (Moon); `rough_rms_lola`, `relief_ellipse` |

### Spectral features pair cleanly with Earth data

The spectral pairs are the strongest `direct` analogies in the set:

- **CRISM ↔ EMIT (VSWIR band depths).** CRISM summary parameters (Viviano-Beck et
  al. 2014: hydration, Fe/Mg- and Al-OH phyllosilicates, sulfates, carbonates) pair
  with **EMIT L2B mineral identification and band depths**: kaolinite, montmorillonite,
  chlorite, gypsum, calcite, dolomite and others. EMIT is CMR collection
  `C2408034484-LPCLOUD` (v001) / `C4079846859-LPCLOUD` (v002), from 2022-08-09.
  Same physics, same parameter type.
- **TES and Diviner ↔ ASTER GED (TIR emissivity).** TES mineral fractions and the
  Diviner Christiansen-feature position pair with the **ASTER Global Emissivity
  Dataset** (AG100 `C2763266348-LPCLOUD`).
- **Caveat on mafic minerals.** EMIT's mineral library was designed for dust-source
  minerals, so olivine/pyroxene (`mafic_bd_1um`) is only a `proxy` until EMIT's
  coverage of those minerals is confirmed.

## 3. Published engineering thresholds (quoted from primary sources)

| Constraint | Mars (MSL guide v2) | Moon (Artemis III) |
|---|---|---|
| Latitude | 60°S–60°N | within 6° of the south pole |
| Elevation | ≤ +2 km vs. MOLA areoid (baseline); −2.0 km contingency. InSight: ≤ −2.5 km | — |
| Slope, descent scale | ≤ 3° over 2–5 km; ≤ 5° over 200–500 m; ≤ 15° over 20–40 m | — |
| Slope, touchdown scale | ≤ 15° over 5 m | < 10° over 100 m (8° continuous-slope contours shown) |
| Slope, surface ops | rover static stability ≥ 45°, nominal ops < 30° tilt; design terrain 15% rocks / 5° mean slope / 10% slip | EVA < 20° over 2 km |
| Rocks | P(rock > 0.6 m in 4 m²) < 0.25% ⇒ ~10% cumulative fractional area | not specified in the probed sources |
| Winds | steady horizontal < 30 m/s, vertical < 10 m/s (last km of descent) | — |
| Surface material | Ka-band backscatter > −20 dB; TI < 100 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻½ **and** albedo > 0.25 ⇒ unsuitable (dust) | — |
| Comms / light | — | direct-with-Earth link (no relay); Earth visibility < 25% and solar visibility < 5% reduce usable area; surface lit for the whole stay; land within 100 m of target |

These are mission engineering limits, not analog tolerances. An Earth test site for
`OP-5LM` (Mars EVA/mobility) needs the slope and rock distribution of the target
zone, not the lander's limits. W5 decides how to turn them into envelopes.
