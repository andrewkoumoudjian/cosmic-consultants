## How to read this file

- Source 1: the [nasa/spaceapps discussions](https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/discussions),
  438 discussions read (100 per page, all pages). Search terms: `analog`,
  `analogue`, `moon base`, `permanent moon base`, `Earth Locations that Analog`,
  `analogue search`, `terrestrial`, `regolith`, `challenge #09`. Discussion
  search also matches comments, so a team that only commented is still found.
- Source 2: GitHub repo search (`gh search repos`) for the same phrases, to
  catch teams that publish code but never post on the board.
- "Repo public?" means anyone can open it. Private repos do not show up in
  any of this, so "no" means "none found in public", not "none exists".

## D. What stands out

- Only one discussion team ships public code: roamer (`sushant-me`). It is the
  only team here with a live app plus a written method, a sensitivity test and
  named control sites. That is the bar to beat on presentation.
- Most other public repos are weekend prototypes: 23 of 27 have no live URL,
  and 5 are empty or near-empty stubs (plus two extra empty repos under
  `Mr-Dante137-tj`). Several were created before the event, so check dates
  before treating them as finished work.
- Only three repos besides roamer are live: Twin World (`TayyabXtreme`),
  Nazeer (`omarmohamed456`) and ATLAS (`abdulhayykhan`). ATLAS names four real
  Moon base sites, which is closer to our framing than a generic similarity map.
- Team Stalred (Guatemala) and the Valparaíso team talk about the challenge but
  have published nothing. They are the ones most likely to appear late.
- Nobody public is doing what we do: no single similarity score, per-trait
  matches, Pareto shortlist, and Lean-checked statements. Our differentiator
  is still open.
